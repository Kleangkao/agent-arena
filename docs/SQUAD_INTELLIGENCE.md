# Squad intelligence and release evidence

27 November 2026. The deterministic `squads-v3` rules, the V7 replay format and the historical master specification are unchanged.

## Agent loop

A guest starts a server-owned match with a locked mode, model, opponent and coaching revision. Both commanders receive the same checkpoint before either plan is applied. The adapter sends the published full board, own slots and health, observable rival positions, current score, objective state and a compact action catalog. Continuous matches now share the engine's geometric pulse targeting for a current eligibility preview. That preview can change as units move. A subsequent request receives the same side's previous accepted orders and authoritative per-slot movement, block, pulse, objective-progress and score evidence. Rival plans and coaching are excluded.

The fixed transport uses one order string per slot plus a required `pulseSlots` array in TDM. Hold and empty authorization remain valid. The result is normalized mechanically and validated against the actual board, life state, mode and slot count. A rejected coordinate can receive nearby verified legal choices and a hold alternative on the one bounded retry. The model must author the replacement. The engine only applies paired valid plans, and the replay records those plans rather than provider output or suggestions. No strategy is generated by the executor. Observed shots, movements, score and Core transitions are shown separately from commentary in the spectator view.

Position and action eligibility are currently easy to compute in the engine but harder to express reliably with generated raw coordinates. Both invalid TDM runs requested a coordinate in the blocked center row on their last retry. This puts the next experiment at the navigation interface, before scheduling or balance changes. A stable bounded list of independently legal destination IDs would let the commander choose without counting columns. It needs a fixed-state comparison against raw coordinates. The current 12-tick paired windows were retained. Early replanning would require time to be represented in the replay and a new fairness policy, and the failure evidence here does not isolate that constraint. Inference remains one call per team per checkpoint, typically 6–8 accepted calls in a completed 5v5 match plus bounded retries.

The TDM reference establishes that explicit pulse authorization can reach the engine and change executed play. Earlier TDM produced a 0–0 replay with no permissions. The two receipt trials do not establish a reliability gain. The Bomb trial supplies a successful correction and genuine Core execution under the latest interface. Its coaching asked Blue to reassess outcomes instead of prescribing a site. There is one sample, and the archived Bomb used different wording, so the effect of general coaching remains unisolated. Relay remains the spatial reference. The 10v10 and 20v20 evidence is deterministic and uses no inference. Squad learning and memory improvements remain unevaluated.
